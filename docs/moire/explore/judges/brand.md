# Palette judge: brand lens

Judge: brand fit and meaning. Date 2026-10-17. Read-only pass. I read the dossier, the research files and all 15 candidate PNGs plus G1 and G2, and I rendered a scratch brand specimen in /tmp/brandjudge (a.png, b.png, c.png). It shows Basteleur at hero size, a glass "loupe" CTA over a two-source ring field, accent links, a focus ring and a hovered row, per palette and per mode. Every candidate passes the contrast contract (see each contrast.txt), so this lens scores meaning, ownership and fit with the moiré only.

## What the brand asks of a palette

1. **Interference / distinct, never separate.** Two things that stay themselves and make a third between them. Ideally the palette has a two-part structure, and the relationship between the parts is the story.
2. **Physicist turned independent dev.** Precision, a real physics reference the owner would enjoy explaining, not a mood board.
3. **Mycelic Wealth warmth.** A person, not an instrument panel. Some warmth must survive the move away from humus and gold.
4. **Calm confidence.** One accent, kept for action. The moiré is the only motif, so the fringe colour must not compete with the accent.
5. **Owner ownership.** He likes Basteleur (archaic, calligraphic, a little alchemical), nerd mono, glass loupe on the primary action, editorial legibility. He hates AI slop (purple-violet buttons are the signature) and generic looks ("pas un truc bateau"). He rejected terminal cosplay (R1), so green or lime on near-black is a risk in dark mode.
6. **Brand architecture.** I checked alysis.cat's tokens (`/data/crqpt/repos/alysis-cat/alysis-cat/src/styles/tokens.css`): cream `#f3ecdd`, ink `#2b2217`, terracotta `#a14a28` = oklch(0.514 0.125 41), moss `#3d5a3d` = oklch(0.436 0.058 144). The atelier already sits in the G1 / sage-cream-terracotta family. So the hub (William Moiré) should not copy alysis, but should feel related to it.

### OPTICS5 Lycurgus: 9.0

- **Meaning.** This is the only candidate whose physics story *is* the brand sentence. One material shows two colours, and which one you see is decided only by the relationship between light and viewer: that is "the pattern lives in the relation", stated as a famous object. It is also the textbook plasmonics example, so a physicist recognises it, and it has the whimsy of a 4th century Roman cup that does nanophysics. The theme toggle becomes the cup: lit from the front or lit from behind.
- **Ownership.** Jade glass and ruby suit Basteleur's archaic, alchemical letterforms better than any neutral grey. In the specimen the ruby glass pill reads as a cut gem, which suits the "loupe" request and stays on topic (glass on glass). The tinted jade-grey ground keeps it editorial rather than clinical.
- **Warmth.** The ruby (hue 5) brings back the warmth that gold carried, without earth. The green-tinted ground keeps it calm.
- **Moiré.** The fringe is drawn in the ground's own jade, not the accent. The accent stays reserved for the one action, which is exactly the budget of one primary action.
- **Siblings.** Jade-grey by day, jade-black by night, the same ruby in both. That gives one brand colour for the OG image, the favicon monogram and the CTA.
- **Brand architecture bonus.** It is alysis transposed: terracotta oklch(0.514 0.125 41) becomes ruby oklch(0.515 0.16 5) at the same lightness, moss 144 becomes jade 172, cream becomes jade glass. Hub and atelier read as relatives without looking cloned. Distinct, never separate, at the brand-system level.
- **Risks.**
  - Red plus green can tip into Christmas if the ground chroma rises. Keep the ground at C ≤ 0.012 (light) and ≤ 0.015 (dark), and never pair saturated jade with the ruby.
  - Ruby collides with "error red" in the intake form. Errors must use ink plus a glyph plus text, not red.
  - Red-green colour blindness: function survives on lightness (accent L 0.51 against ink L 0.28), but links need underlines.
  - The dark fill is only 3.43:1 on the page and the white label 5.08:1. A translucent glass fill would break contrast, so the glass has to live in the rim and highlight, with the fill at least 92% opaque.

### SPORE4 Érythrite: 8.0

- **Structure.** Almost the same as OPTICS5: green-grey nickel paper, teal-black night, crimson cobalt-bloom action. Two independent lenses converging on jade plus ruby is itself a signal that this is the strongest region.
- **Why it loses.**
  - The accent drifts to hue 352. In the render it reads raspberry-magenta and a little purple, which is closer to the AI-magenta zone and cooler (less Mycelic warmth).
  - The paper is mintier (C 0.011), which leans pharmacy.
  - The fringe is neutral ink, which is correct but has less charm than OPTICS5's jade fringe.
  - The story (a continuous solid-solution series) is true and on message, but needs a chemistry lesson. Lycurgus explains itself in one sentence.
- **Worth keeping.** Its slightly deeper teal-black night and its ink-fringe fallback.

### PRINT5 Deux encres: 6.5

- **Strengths.** The strongest *structural* echo of the wordmark: the two inks swap roles between modes, the way W rotates into M. The Riso misregistration story literally explains printed moiré. Its coloured ground in both modes is non-generic.
- **Weaknesses.**
  - It has no single brand colour (is William Moiré plum or mint?), which complicates the OG image, favicon and social avatars.
  - The plum CTA by day borders on purple, though it is muted.
  - The mint-tinted body text on plum in dark mode is less comfortable for long reading, and the pastel mint pill turns milky under glass.
  - Riso is a design-scene trend, not the owner's world.
- **If chosen.** Lock plum as the asset colour and use mint only as the dark-mode action.

### OPTICS3 Antireflet: 6.5

- **Strengths.**
  - It answers the owner's "not eye-tiring" more directly than any other candidate, and with physics: an AR coating is interference used for comfort, and everyone has seen the green-magenta glint on their glasses.
  - Its structure is the best in the set: dimmed page, lifted night, raised rows *lighter* than the page, ink near 11.5:1.
  - Drawing the moiré in the magenta residue gives the "third pattern" its own colour.
- **Weaknesses.** Brand-wise it splits identity: a green action plus a magenta focus ring read as two accents in the specimen. The deep sea-green fill leans bank or fintech, and there is little warmth.
- **Use.** Graft its structure onto the winner.

### SPORE1 Strophaire vert-de-gris: 6.5

- **Strengths.** Aubergine ink on mauve-grey paper is the most beautiful Basteleur rendering in the set: it looks like real ink on tinted stock. The story (one organism, distinct parts) maps onto "identity is the network, projects are its organs".
- **Weaknesses.** The verdigris action is the generic dev teal that the spore lens itself rejected for slate. The mauve paper can read cosmetic. The fringe is the accent colour, which dilutes the action.

### PRINT4 Tableau noir: 6.0

- **Strengths.** Very on-meaning: physicist, teacher (Plan ₿ Academy), equations worked out in the open (« à ciel ouvert »). The inverted button, a small board with chalk on it in light mode, is charming. The chalk-dust fringe in ink colour keeps the accent reserved.
- **Weaknesses.**
  - In dark mode, a dark ground with a yellow pill sits uncomfortably close to G1 dark's gold pill. Lemon hue 103 against gold hue 77 is only 26 degrees apart.
  - The green slate is in mockup A's moss family.
  - Blackboard nostalgia risks a schoolroom feel.

### OPTICS4 555 nm: 5.5

- **Strengths.** The story is the wittiest physicist fact in the set. The light mode (lacquered dark button with a chartreuse label) is elegant.
- **Weaknesses.** Lit lime on graphite in dark mode is phosphor-terminal adjacent (R1) and is the 2025 to 2026 AI-startup lime trope. It is cold, with no Mycelic warmth. A lime fringe tints the field green.

### SPORE3 Lichen géographique: 5.5

- **Strengths.** The best biological analogue of the brand: a lichen is a fungus and an alga making a third organism. It is anchored above Chambéry and measured with a ruler (lichenometry). I would steal the story for copy.
- **Weaknesses.** The palette is blue-black slate plus one electric signal, which is G2's structure with lime swapped for cobalt, and it carries the same lime and terminal risk as OPTICS4.

### OPTICS2 Labradorite: 5.0

- **Strengths.** A good interference story, and one petrol fill kept constant in both modes, which is good brand discipline.
- **Weaknesses.** Petrol on warm grey reads consultancy, health SaaS or bank: correct, but "bateau". It sits in the cool blue family, 55 degrees from cobalt.

### PRINT3 Vert-de-gris: 5.0

- **Strengths.** The copper conductor story (circulation) is apt, and the copper-hairline fringe under a patina accent is a lovely two-hue moiré idea.
- **Weaknesses.** Visibly it is teal on pewter: generic dev-theme teal, nearly identical in feel to OPTICS2.

### PRINT1 Teinte sensible: 5.0

- **Strengths.** It holds the best single idea in the print lens: magenta has no wavelength of its own and exists only in the relation between red and blue, exactly as the moiré belongs to neither layer.
- **Weaknesses.** Neutral graphite plus one hot signal is the op-art-plus-signal structure named as a G2 cousin. The C 0.19 magenta pill reads fashion, design tool or synthwave in dark mode. This is a story to quote, not a palette to ship.

### SPORE2 Rhodochrosite: 4.5

- **Strengths.** It is the warmest candidate, and its concentric bands rhyme with the é-source ring field. The wine-dark night is beautiful.
- **Weaknesses.**
  - Monochrome rose drops the two-layer duality.
  - Blush paper with a raspberry pill reads beauty, wellness or wedding stationery. That clashes with "sharp, professional physicist" and sits near the wellness register the dossier keeps away from (risk list #3).
  - The pastel pink pill in dark mode is candy.

### OPTICS1 Teinte sensible: 4.0

- **Strength.** A tinted lilac ground avoids PRINT1's op-art structure.
- **Weakness.** The orchid-fuchsia pill in both modes lands in AI-purple territory, and the lilac paper reads cosmetic. It is the weakest expression of an idea that PRINT1 frames better.

### SPORE5 Améthyste dans la mousse: 3.5 (veto)

- **Strength.** The circulation story is on message.
- **Weaknesses.** A saturated violet night (C 0.035) with a lime pill is a code-editor theme look (Dracula or Synthwave), loud and complementary. Violet is the AI-default family. A coloured violet ground also tires long reading.

### PRINT2 Encre violette: 2.5 (veto)

- **Strength.** The Seyès notebook is a charming, very French story, and its ruling really is a grating.
- **Weaknesses.** A violet-600 pill on cool white is the single most recognisable AI-slop signature. Hue 298 on cool white also sits next to G2's indigo (hue 267).
- **Keep.** Only the idea of drawing the fringe in a second "ruling" hue.

## Graft ideas (onto OPTICS5)

1. **Make the moiré enact the cup.** The fringe is jade by day (as now) and ruby by night, for example `oklch(0.70 0.12 5 / 0.05)` in dark (1.07:1 luminance delta on the night bg, inside the moiré budget). The theme toggle is then literally "front-lit or back-lit". The accent fill stays ruby in both modes, so the brand colour never changes.
2. **OPTICS3's comfort structure.**
   - Lift the night from L 0.195 to about 0.205 to 0.208. That keeps ink at about 12.7:1 and the ruby fill at about 3.5:1. Do not lift to 0.218: the fill would fall to 3.26:1.
   - Dim the page slightly (0.957 to about 0.950).
   - Make raised rows *lighter* than the page in light mode, so hover lifts instead of sinking.
3. **Hold the ruby at hue 5 to 8.** Do not drift to SPORE4's 352: that is the magenta zone, cooler and more AI-like. If the dark white-on-ruby label needs headroom, use a fill of `oklch(0.53 0.165 6)` (5.53:1 for the label).
4. **Glass loupe recipe for this palette.** The fill stays at least 92% opaque. Put the glass in a 1 px inner highlight, a rim refraction of the moiré and a soft ruby shadow, never a translucent body (on-accent margins are only 5.1 to 5.9:1).
5. **Error states.** Never ruby. Use ink plus a glyph plus a sentence. Ruby means "act here", nothing else.
6. **SPORE4's ink-coloured fringe** as a fallback if the jade fringe looks muddy on some light-mode screens.
7. **Copy, not colour.** The theme-toggle tooltip or the 404 can borrow the Lycurgus line. PRINT1's "a colour with no wavelength of its own" or SPORE3's lichen line can serve as manifesto or footnote material. None of them on home.
8. **If PRINT5 is preferred instead.** Lock plum as the single asset colour. Neutralise the dark-mode body ink to C ≤ 0.01. Keep mint only for the dark action.
